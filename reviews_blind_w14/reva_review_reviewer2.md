# Review of "REVA: Region-Evidence Arbitration with Vocabulary-Adaptive Background Synthesis for Vocabulary-Robust Training-free Open-Vocabulary Segmentation"

**Reviewer 2 — focus: experimental design, external validity, reproducibility. Independent blind review.**

---

## Summary

The paper addresses the "plain-vocabulary gap" in training-free OVSS: when the engineered benchmark vocabularies (background query expansion, positive sub-class expansion) are replaced by plain class names, methods such as MaskCLIP/SCLIP/ClearCLIP/NACLIP lose 12–21 mIoU. REVA is a training-free plug-in with two components: (i) **VABS** — facility-location selection of M=64 background negatives from a fixed 168-entry scene/stuff lexicon (COCO-Stuff ∪ ADE-150 names), with a cosine-similarity safety filter (τ=0.90); and (ii) **region-evidence arbitration** — averaging per-pixel class probabilities inside SAM ViT-B automasks and re-assigning each region by pooled evidence, with pixel fallback. On a dev-excluded VOC-21 split, REVA lifts four base methods by +19.9 to +23.1 mIoU from the plain baseline, closing 86–96% of the gap to a matched-compute engineered-vocabulary upper bound (official + SAM), and adds +4 to +9 on top of the official vocabulary. The paper reports a pre-registered evidence chain with kill criteria (two killed stages disclosed), a self-attack scalar background-boost control, matched random-negative controls, transfer to COCO-Object / Context-60 / ADE-150 / ViT-L / three additional 2024–25-style base reimplementations (ProxyCLIP-, LPOSS-, SC-CLIP-style), same-protocol anchors against PAMR and Trident's SAM-refinement stage, and a preliminary detector-guided vocabulary-pruning third component (OWLv2).

## Strengths

1. **Unusually rigorous experimental hygiene.** Pre-registration with frozen kill criteria, disclosed killed stages (SLIC pooling, DINOv2 visual veto, learned text-side adapter, visual-side adapter, routing gate), a dev/test split with dev images excluded from all reported numbers, dataset-level mIoU from a globally aggregated confusion matrix (avoiding the GT-present per-image artifact), and per-cell run provenance (App. B). This is well above the field's norm.
2. **Matched controls that actually isolate the mechanism.** The matched random-negatives arm, the matched-compute upper bound (official + SAM), the tuned scalar background-boost self-attack (with its cross-dataset transfer failure), the hand-written 26-entry background control (including its off-VOC transplant), and the LaVG-style feature-pooling control together give a credible decomposition: most of VABS = automatic background re-calibration; residual +2.8–4.4 = selection; region arbitration ≈ competitive with, not superior to, other SAM/affinity mechanisms.
3. **Honest negative-space reporting.** The paper explicitly reports: engineered vocabulary retains a 0.9–3.6 lead at matched compute; PAMR matches SAM arbitration on the official vocabulary at ~1/5 the cost; feature pooling ties probability pooling; hand-written background matches VABS on VOC; Context-60/ADE-150 nulls for negative selection; residual person/tvmonitor harm (Table 3, Fig. 3); SAM dominates runtime (~12× the CLIP pass). Framing the contribution as "automation and vocabulary robustness, not a superior mechanism" is accurate and refreshing.
4. **Breadth of base methods.** Seven bases spanning attention-surgery (4), proxy-attention, anomaly-restoration, and label-propagation families, plus an external anchor on the *unmodified* official ProxyCLIP release (47.3 → 56.2 by swapping the name file only) — a genuinely convincing external-validity check for the text-side component.
5. **Runtime disclosure with hardware.** Table 2 gives per-mechanism s/img (PAMR 0.08, Trident-refinement 0.13, REVA automask 0.42, CLIP pass 0.035; RTX 3090), which most SAM-based OVSS papers omit.

## Weaknesses

1. **The central protocol is self-built, and the paper's headline deltas may not survive publication-grade configurations.** All main results use a deliberately minimal protocol (no PAMR, no multi-scale/flip), yielding baselines far below published numbers (NACLIP official 56.9 vs published 64.1). The paper's own anchor shows that under the official vocabulary, cheap PAMR already reaches 62.6 = SAM arbitration. The key unanswered external-validity question: **how large is the plain→REVA gain when the base method runs its full published pipeline (PAMR + its tuned settings)?** If PAMR absorbs part of what region arbitration provides in the VABS regime too (the paper shows the SAM edge over PAMR shrinks to +0.7 with VABS), the practical headroom of REVA's expensive component (0.42 s/img) in a real deployment is unclear. A full-pipeline column (published config ± REVA) is needed.
2. **Factual inconsistency in the reproduction-gap explanation.** The paper attributes the 62.6-vs-64.1 NACLIP gap to "resolution/multi-scale" (§4.5) and earlier says the published pipeline "uses higher resolution and PAMR" (§4). I checked NACLIP's paper/repo: its published VOC-21 = 64.07 uses short side **336, 224 window, stride 112 — identical to this paper's protocol —** plus PAMR. There is no resolution difference on VOC; the residual ~1.5 must come from something else (vocabulary/template details, implementation). This is a small but real accuracy problem in a paper whose brand is meticulousness, and it makes me want the run records checked.
3. **Cross-dataset coverage is asymmetric and partly under-powered.** VOC-21 gets the full split and four methods; COCO-Object gets two methods; ADE-150 and ViT-L are 300-image subsets; the three new-generation bases are test-300 only; random-negative controls at full scale are single-seed. Several claims that matter for external validity (ViT-L selection advantage "method-dependent", SC-CLIP/LPOSS per-class harm figures, detector-pruning) rest on 300-image single-seed cells with no variance estimates. Cityscapes and COCO-Stuff — standard in every baseline's evaluation suite — are absent entirely; both are low-background-room regimes where the paper's own model predicts near-null gains, so their absence looks like (probably unintentional) selective benchmarking. Report them; the boundary-condition story would be strengthened, not weakened, by predicted nulls landing.
4. **Positioning relative to Trident and TCC is mostly fair but incomplete.**
   - *Trident* (Shi et al., ICCV 2025; arXiv 2411.09219 — verified): the anchor ports only Trident's SAM-refinement stage onto this paper's dense probabilities, and honestly discloses that full Trident (CLIP+DINO splicing, SAM ViT-H, higher resolution; 48.6 avg over 8 benchmarks) is not reproduced. Fair as far as it goes — but the anchor shows the prompt-based decoder pass is ~3× cheaper and within ±0.7 mIoU. The paper should state plainly in the abstract/intro (not only §4.5) that its SAM-usage mechanism is *not* preferred on cost-adjusted grounds; the current top-line framing still leads with region arbitration as a named contribution.
   - *TCC*: the comparison is deferred to future work, yet TCC is the closest text-side neighbor (test-time contrastive concepts derived per query). The paper's distinction (multi-class covering set vs single-concept contrastive concepts) is real, but TCC's LLM/co-occurrence-generated negatives could plausibly be adapted to the multi-class plain-vocabulary setting as a baseline for VABS with modest effort; its absence leaves the "vocabulary-conditioned selection" novelty claim resting only on the random-negatives and static-list controls. Also a citation error: reference [17] attributes TCC to "W. Bousselham et al." — the paper is **Wysoczańska et al.** (TMLR 2025, arXiv 2407.05061 — verified; Bousselham is not among the authors). Fix.
   - LPOSS (Stojnic et al., CVPR 2025) and SC-CLIP (Bai et al., TIP 2025) are used as "style-reimplementation" bases but are **not cited in the references**, and "style-reimplementation" is never defined precisely enough to reproduce (which components were reimplemented, what was validated against the originals?). This is a reproducibility gap for a third of the base methods.
5. **Residual per-class harm is disclosed but not solved, and the safety criterion is chosen conveniently.** The pre-registered safety criterion compares REVA vs *pixel-level VABS* ("arbitration adds no harm"), while the user-relevant comparison is vs the *plain baseline*, where person (−6 to −9 on 3/4 methods) and tvmonitor (−3 to −12) remain harmed, and worse on the new-generation bases (person up to −15.4, LPOSS tvmonitor → 2.1). The paper says so plainly — credit — but the abstract's "recovers the gap for arbitrary plain vocabularies" oversells given two of 21 VOC classes regress materially, and the mechanism prediction of *which* classes get hurt on a new vocabulary is absent. A practitioner cannot know a priori whether their "person"-analog is at risk.
6. **Lexicon leakage on COCO-Object weakens the strongest transfer result.** Disclosed, but not quantified: an ablation removing COCO-Stuff names from the lexicon (ADE-only lexicon) on COCO-Object would bound the leak in one run. Without it, the +12/+13.9 COCO transfer cannot be taken at face value.
7. **Dev split provenance.** Hyper-parameters (M=64, τ=0.90) were fixed "in earlier pre-registered work" on 100 VOC images and never re-tuned — good — but the dev images come from the same distribution as the main benchmark, and cross-dataset hyper-parameter sensitivity is untested (acknowledged in §5). At minimum a sensitivity sweep of M and τ on one transfer dataset should be reported.
8. **§4.6 (detector pruning) does not belong in this paper at its current maturity.** Single-seed, 300-image subsets, self-labeled "preliminary", imports grounding supervision (contradicting the training-free framing), and adds a page of dense caveats. It dilutes an otherwise tight paper; move to the appendix or the companion audit.

## Questions

1. What is the plain→REVA delta when each base runs its **full published pipeline** (PAMR, method-specific settings)? Does region arbitration retain any edge over PAMR-on-VABS at published configurations, given Table 2 shows only +0.7 at the minimal protocol?
2. The NACLIP reproduction gap: published NACLIP uses the same 336/224/112 protocol as yours. What specifically accounts for the residual 62.6 vs 64.1 after PAMR — and can you correct the "resolution/multi-scale" attribution?
3. Can you run the ADE-only (COCO-Stuff-free) lexicon on COCO-Object to bound the leakage effect claimed in §4.4?
4. Why are Cityscapes and COCO-Stuff excluded? Your boundary-condition model makes testable predictions there.
5. What exactly is a "style-reimplementation" of ProxyCLIP/LPOSS/SC-CLIP — which components, validated how against the authors' code? Why is the author-code anchor available for ProxyCLIP only?
6. For a new user vocabulary, can VABS's safety filter (or any signal you tested) *predict* which target classes will be harmed (the person/tvmonitor analogs), rather than only measuring the harm post hoc on labelled benchmarks?
7. Multi-seed random-negative controls at full-split scale: the +4.7–5.9 selection advantage is single-seed; what is the seed variance?
8. SAM points-per-side is 16; how sensitive are the results (and runtime) to grid density, and does a cheaper grid preserve the gain?

## Ratings

- **Soundness:** 3.5/4 — internally rigorous; minor factual slip (Q2) and single-seed cells.
- **Contribution/Novelty:** 2.5/4 — mechanism components individually known (acknowledged); value is the combination, the automation claim, and the evidence chain.
- **Presentation:** 3/4 — dense but disciplined; §4.6 sprawls; missing citations for two base methods.
- **Overall: 5.5/10 — Weak Accept (borderline).**
- **Confidence: 4/5.** I verified Trident (arXiv 2411.09219, ICCV 2025), TCC (arXiv 2407.05061, Wysoczańska et al., TMLR 2025), LPOSS (arXiv 2503.19777, CVPR 2025), SC-CLIP (arXiv 2411.15869, TIP 2025), and NACLIP's published protocol/results; I did not re-run any code.

**Verdict.** The experimental discipline is exemplary and the vocabulary-robustness problem is real and under-studied. What holds the paper at borderline is external validity: all headline deltas live inside a self-built minimal protocol whose relationship to publication-grade pipelines is only partially anchored, the expensive component (SAM automasks) is shown by the paper's own controls to be cost-dominated by PAMR/prompt-refinement in most regimes, and the residual per-class harm means the "arbitrary plain vocabularies" claim is not fully delivered.

## What separates this from a strong accept

1. **Full-pipeline validation**: plain ± REVA under each method's published configuration (PAMR, tuned resolution), showing the gain survives — this is the single highest-leverage addition.
2. **A TCC-adapted baseline** for VABS (LLM/co-occurrence contrastive concepts extended to multi-class), completing the nearest-neighbor comparison the paper itself flags.
3. **Complete the benchmark suite** (Cityscapes, COCO-Stuff) to confirm the boundary-condition model's predicted nulls, and the ADE-only-lexicon COCO run to bound leakage.
4. **Multi-seed controls** at full-split scale with variance, and full-split (not test-300) results for the three new-generation bases.
5. **A predictive per-class safety story**: any a-priori signal for which classes a new vocabulary will harm — even a partial one — would convert the disclosed limitation into a usable tool.
6. **Hygiene fixes**: correct the TCC citation (Wysoczańska et al., not Bousselham); cite LPOSS and SC-CLIP; fix the resolution attribution for the NACLIP reproduction gap; define "style-reimplementation" reproducibly (or release the reimplementations).
7. **Cost-adjusted framing up front**: lead with "VABS + any refinement (PAMR/Trident-refinement/SAM pooling)" as the recipe, since the paper's own Table 2 shows the arbitration mechanism is interchangeable — the durable contribution is VABS plus the evidence chain, and the paper is stronger when it says so from the abstract.
